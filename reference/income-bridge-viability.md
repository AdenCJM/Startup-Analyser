# Income-Bridge Viability Framework

**Purpose:** evaluate ideas as income bridges, things that generate revenue to extend your runway while you build the primary bet. The target is not full income replacement; it is durable, predictable income that buys runway and reduces the pressure to raise or exit prematurely.

It sits alongside the other lenses:

- **Founder Constraints** answers "can I build this, given who I am?"
- **Market Opportunity** answers "is this worth building, regardless of founder?"
- **Runway & Sequencing** answers "can I start this now, does it use my edge, and can it reach revenue within my runway?"
- **Income-Bridge Viability** answers "what best extends my runway alongside the primary bet?"

The difference from Runway & Sequencing: that lens asks "what is the primary vine, and what order do I swing in?" This lens asks "what generates the best risk-adjusted income to keep me airborne while I build the primary bet?"

---

## Why this framework exists

The other lenses converge on the primary bet: build the best venture and reach a fundable, revenue-generating milestone. But a founder on finite runway has a second, parallel need: income that extends the runway without consuming the focus the primary bet demands.

This changes which ideas look attractive:

- An idea killed as a primary venture (low market ceiling) might be an excellent income bridge.
- Revenue predictability matters more than raw ceiling. Runway stability beats a volatile spike.
- Time to revenue matters more than total addressable market. The bridge has to pay soon.
- The bridge must not cannibalise the primary bet's focus (see the Income-Bridge Trap below).

---

## The three dimensions

Score each 1-5. Justify in one line. Calculate the weighted composite. Every idea is scored, including ones killed as primary ventures.

### 1. Time to Revenue (Weight: 30%)

How long from zero to first paying customer, at the pace you can give it alongside the primary bet?

| Score | Definition |
|---|---|
| 5 | First dollar within 4 weeks. Launchable in a short sprint. An existing audience or network provides immediate customers. |
| 4 | First dollar within 2-3 months. A clear path from build to launch. The product can be scoped small enough to ship fast. |
| 3 | First dollar within 4-6 months. A meaningful build phase before anything is sellable. |
| 2 | First dollar within 6-12 months. Significant build, audience-building, or domain learning before revenue is possible. |
| 1 | 12+ months before realistic revenue. Long sales cycles, compliance requirements, or a large build phase. |

**Ask:** if you started this week, when does the first customer pay?

### 2. Income Ceiling (Weight: 40%)

Realistic monthly revenue achievable while the primary bet stays the main focus, that is, without the bridge demanding full-time attention.

| Score | Definition |
|---|---|
| 5 | $10K+/month achievable as a bridge. No structural ceiling at part-attention effort. A scalable product with a high price point or high volume. |
| 4 | $5-10K/month achievable. A clear revenue model with good unit economics. Growth continues without a proportional time increase. |
| 3 | $2-5K/month achievable. The ceiling is real, but the income is a meaningful runway extension. |
| 2 | $500-2K/month. Modest income relative to effort. A marginal runway impact. |
| 1 | Under $500/month, or structurally requires full-time attention to generate meaningful revenue. Not viable as a bridge. |

**Ask:** at 100 customers paying the expected price, what is monthly revenue, and does serving them steal attention the primary bet needs?

### 3. Revenue Predictability (Weight: 30%)

How stable and recurring is the income once established? For a runway bridge this matters more than ceiling: a predictable $3K/month extends runway more reliably than a volatile $0-15K/month.

| Score | Definition |
|---|---|
| 5 | Monthly recurring revenue. Subscriptions or usage-based billing with low churn. Next month is about 95% of this month even with zero new sales. |
| 4 | A repeat-purchase pattern. Not subscription, but customers come back regularly. Predictable within a band. Seasonal variation is manageable. |
| 3 | A mixed model. Some recurring revenue, some one-time sales. Fluctuates month to month but trends upward. |
| 2 | Mostly one-time purchases or launch-driven revenue. Resets each month. Lumpy and seasonal. Needs constant marketing to maintain. |
| 1 | Project-based or event-driven. Revenue depends on winning each engagement individually. Long gaps between payments. |

**Ask:** if you stop marketing for a month, does revenue continue? Can you predict next month's revenue within 20%?

---

## Composite score

**Formula:** `(Time to Revenue x 0.30) + (Income Ceiling x 0.40) + (Revenue Predictability x 0.30)`

The weights are load-bearing. Income Ceiling carries the most weight (40%) because the bridge has to generate *meaningful* runway extension to be worth any focus at all. Time to Revenue and Revenue Predictability are weighted equally (30% each): the bridge has to pay soon *and* pay reliably. A slow bridge does not help a finite runway, and a volatile bridge does not reduce raise pressure.

The weighted composite runs on a **1.0 to 5.0 scale** (the weights sum to 1.0), so the bands below sit on that same scale.

**Bands:**

| Composite | Label | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| >= 4.0 | **Excellent** | A high-priority income bridge. Strong, durable income with low focus cost. |
| 3.0 - 3.9 | **Viable** | Worth considering. Real runway extension, with meaningful trade-offs. |
| 2.0 - 2.9 | **Marginal** | Significant drawbacks. Income is modest, slow, or volatile. |
| < 2.0 | **Not Viable** | Not worth the focus it draws from the primary bet. |

**Warning flag:** if any single dimension scores 1, flag it prominently regardless of the composite. A 1 on any dimension is a structural problem the composite can mask.

## How this lens interacts with the others

### The two stacks

The venture stack (Founder Constraints + Market Opportunity + Runway & Sequencing) determines the primary bet. The income-bridge stack determines what runs alongside it to extend runway. These are additive, not competing, as long as the bridge respects the focus boundary (see the Income-Bridge Trap).

### Status coexistence

An idea can carry a dual status:

- **KILL (as a venture) + Viable (as an income bridge):** the venture economics do not work, but it generates useful runway-extending income. It stays Killed as a venture and appears in the income-bridge priority stack.
- **BUILD (as a venture) + Excellent (as an income bridge):** the best case. A strong income bridge that is also a credible standalone build.
- **OPPORTUNITY GAP (as a venture) + Not Viable (as an income bridge):** a big market, wrong circumstances on both axes. Watch for constraint-flip conditions.

### Practical application

The income-bridge priority stack fills the **Career Capital Builder** slot in the Runway & Sequencing vine-to-vine sequence. When the sequence says "run an income bridge alongside the primary bet," this stack chooses *which* specific product to build first.

## The Income-Bridge Trap

**Warning:** a successful income bridge can become a golden cage.

If a bridge generates comfortable, predictable income, it creates psychological inertia against the harder primary bet. The bridge income feels safer and more immediate than the uncertain path to a venture-scale outcome. You stop pushing on the primary bet because the bridge "covers the runway" and the primary bet is hard.

**Guardrail:** the bridge runs in the Career Capital Builder slot, with bounded focus, never the main allocation. If it starts consuming the focus the primary bet needs, or if you catch yourself deprioritising the primary bet because the bridge is "good enough," that is the trap activating.

**The test:** if you removed the bridge income tomorrow, would you still be building the primary bet? If yes, the bridge is additive. If no, it is a crutch.

---

## Scoring all ideas

Unlike the other lenses, Income-Bridge Viability evaluates every idea in the portfolio, including ones killed as ventures. "Should I build this as the primary business?" and "is this a good source of runway-extending income?" are different questions. An idea can be a bad venture and a good income bridge, or the reverse.

---

## A note on the default form

The three-dimension form shipped here assumes a **full-time founder on runway**, with no employer and no day job to manage around. That is why two dimensions a different reader might expect are deliberately absent:

- There is no employer-compatibility gate. With no employer, there is no conflict-of-interest filter to apply.
- There is no day-job-synergy dimension. With no day job, there is nothing to synergise with.

If your situation is different, for example you are employed and want to run something on the side, you can reinstate a fourth dimension and reweight the four to sum to 100%. That is a reasonable customisation. The shipped default is the three-dimension form, because it matches the full-time-founder case the rest of this system assumes.

*Changelog: this lens was previously called "Side-Hustle Viability" and assumed an employed founder with spare evenings. It was reframed for a full-time founder on finite runway, the employer-compatibility gate and day-job-synergy dimension were dropped, and the three surviving dimensions were reweighted to 30 / 40 / 30.*
