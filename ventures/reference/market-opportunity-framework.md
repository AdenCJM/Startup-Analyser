# Market Opportunity (Venture-Scale)

**Track: venture.** This is the venture-scale Market Opportunity framework. It is the SAME instrument as the solo [Market Opportunity Framework](../../reference/market-opportunity-framework.md): the same 5 dimensions, the same 1-5 scale, the same simple-average composite, the same `>=4.0 HIGH / 2.5-3.9 MED / <2.5 LOW` bands. Only the anchors are calibrated for venture scale. Where the solo doc asks "how valuable is this as a business?", this doc asks the same question through a venture lens: "is this a real venture-scale market bet, large enough to return a fund?"

Market Opportunity here is **founder-agnostic**. It scores whether the market, the moat, the window, and the value-capture are real and valuable *in the abstract*. It does not ask whether this particular venture can capture them. That second question belongs to Venture Feasibility (see the handoff note at the end).

> **Make it yours.** The Market Size dollar bands below are a default. If your category has different norms, adjust the bands, but keep the venture-scale floor: a market that cannot support a fund-returning outcome is a structural warning even when the composite passes.

---

## The Five Dimensions

Score each 1-5. No half points. One-line justification each.

### 1. Problem Severity

How acute is the pain? Is it a burning, named problem buyers are actively trying to solve, or a nice-to-have nobody is writing requirements for?

| Score | Definition |
|---|---|
| 5 | Acknowledged, urgent problem. Buyers are actively seeking solutions and would describe it unprompted. It costs them real money or risk today. |
| 4 | Recognised problem with a clear cost. Buyers want it solved, but the urgency is planned rather than burning. |
| 3 | Real friction that buyers work around. An acknowledged inefficiency, but tolerable with current tools and nobody pushing hard. |
| 2 | A marginal improvement to an already-served need. Nice-to-have; nobody is writing requirements for it. |
| 1 | A capability looking for a problem. A technology in search of a pain buyers do not feel. |

**Ask:** Could you point to a buyer naming this pain unprompted, in their own words?

### 2. Market Size (venture-scale TAM)

How large is the realistic addressable market? It must be able to support a fund-returning outcome.

| Score | Definition |
|---|---|
| 5 | Multi-billion addressable. A category, not a line item, with cross-segment demand. |
| 4 | $1B+ addressable, with credible adjacent expansion. A large market or capability area. |
| 3 | $100M-$1B. A real but bounded niche, single-segment or single-geography. |
| 2 | $10M-$100M. A niche with a hard ceiling. **Below the venture-scale floor.** |
| 1 | Under $10M. A bespoke or one-off market, not a venture market. |

**Venture-scale floor.** A Market Size of **2 or below is flagged as a structural warning even when the composite passes.** A VC-backed, fund-returning outcome requires a market that can scale. A small, severe, well-timed pain can still be a real problem worth a contract, but it is not, on its own, a venture. Note the flag explicitly in the idea README rather than letting a strong Problem Severity or Timing score average it away.

### 3. Defensibility

Can the moat survive a determined, well-funded competitor for roughly five years? Sources of moat: proprietary data, network effects, switching cost, a regulatory or certification position, brand, incumbency.

| Score | Definition |
|---|---|
| 5 | Multiple stacked, durable moats (for example: a data flywheel + network effects + high switching cost). Displacement requires more than a feature. |
| 4 | One durable structural moat plus support: a regulatory or certification position, or accumulated data, that takes a competitor years to match. Hard, slow, and expensive to dislodge. |
| 3 | Relationship and workflow lock-in. Switchable, but switching is painful and costly to the customer. |
| 2 | First-mover and integration advantage only. A funded competitor could match it within a cycle. |
| 1 | A commodity capability in a contested field. A funded competitor or an incumbent absorbs it as a feature. |

**Ask:** If a well-funded incumbent built this next quarter, what specifically stops your customers leaving?

### 4. Timing

Is there a tailwind, real budget or demand, AND an open buying window, at the same time?

| Score | Definition |
|---|---|
| 5 | Tailwind active AND demand AND an open window: buyers are buying now. |
| 4 | Two of three present and the third arriving soon. Strong tailwind and real demand; the buying motion is forming. |
| 3 | The tailwind is real but budget and the buying mechanism lag. A sound thesis, interested buyers, no open path yet. |
| 2 | Speculative. It depends on a shift (a regulation, a budget line, a behaviour change) that may not land soon. |
| 1 | The tailwind is absent or the window is closed: too early (no demand) or too late (an incumbent has locked it). |

**Ask:** If you launched tomorrow, would real buyers pay within 30 days, or are you waiting on demand that does not yet exist?

### 5. Value Capture

Can revenue be captured proportional to the value delivered? Pricing power, recurring or sticky revenue, healthy defensible margins.

| Score | Definition |
|---|---|
| 5 | Sticky, recurring revenue with real pricing power. Customers pay for ongoing value, not a one-off, and margins are defensible. |
| 4 | A clear path to recurring revenue with healthy, defensible margins once on contract. |
| 3 | Value is real but captured through project or pilot money rather than a standing line. Revenue exists but is renewal-dependent and lumpy. |
| 2 | Pilot- or grant-funded with no committed path to durable revenue. Capture depends on a decision outside your control. |
| 1 | Interest without budget ("send us a free pilot"). Enthusiasm that no spending line stands behind. |

**Ask:** Can the customer calculate the ROI on a napkin, and is there a standing budget the revenue sits on?

## Composite Score

Simple average of all 5 dimensions. No weighting. All five matter roughly equally for "is this a good venture-scale market bet?"

| Composite | Band | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| >= 4.0 | **HIGH** | A genuinely large, real, and live opportunity. If the venture fails Venture Feasibility or Venture Constraints, it goes to WATCH rather than PASS. |
| 2.5 - 3.9 | **MED** | A solid opportunity with real ceilings or timing risk. |
| < 2.5 | **LOW** | Structurally weak. Not a venture-scale bet regardless of fit. |

**Read the Market Size flag alongside the band.** A composite can land MED or even HIGH while Market Size sits at 2: a severe, well-timed pain in a small market. The band does not absolve the floor; surface both.

---

## The >= 4.0 Override

A Market Opportunity composite `>=4.0 (HIGH)` cannot, on its own, make a Master Verdict of PURSUE, but it does stop a failing idea from being thrown away. An otherwise-failing idea (a blocked feasibility, or a fragile partnership) with HIGH market and Ethics PASS goes to **WATCH**, not PASS, with Constraint Flip Conditions documented. The highest-Market WATCH idea in the portfolio is the **Crown Jewel**: monitored, signal-tracked, and reviewed first when conditions shift. Full precedence is in the cascade in `venture-feasibility.md`.

## Handoff to Venture Feasibility

Market Opportunity and Venture Feasibility ask two different questions about the same facts, and a fact is never scored twice.

- **Market Opportunity** (this doc) scores whether a market, a moat, a window, and value-capture are real and valuable **in the abstract**, founder-agnostic. Is there a durable moat to be had? Is the window open? Is the money real? It does not ask who captures it.
- **Venture Feasibility** scores whether **this venture** can clear the capital, legal, go-to-market, and build machinery to actually capture that value. It resolves to CLEARED / CONDITIONAL / BLOCKED, with a binary Ethics / Legal veto.

So Defensibility (here) asks "is there a moat in this market?" Venture Feasibility asks "can this venture stand up and hold the position to occupy it?" without re-scoring whether the moat exists. Whether the founders' specific edge is an *unfair advantage* on this idea is scored in exactly one place: Partnership Fit > Combined Founder-Market Fit. Not here, and not in Venture Feasibility.
