# Venture Constraints (Track: venture)

**Use these seven constraints as the condition-layer scoring filter for any `Track: venture` idea: VC-backed, with a co-founder, on a full-time leap.** This file replaces [`reference/founder-constraints.md`](../../reference/founder-constraints.md) for venture-track ideas. Do **not** apply both sets to one idea. The solo six and the venture seven encode opposite conditions, and running both produces contradictory scores (Bootstrap FAIL on the solo side, Bootstrap-required PASS on the venture side, for the same fundable company).

The inversion, in one paragraph: the solo framework optimises for a bootstrapped, side-built, founder-owned asset that throws off steady cash flow and never needs permission. The venture condition optimises for the opposite shape. **Capital is required, not avoided** (a venture-fundable company must take capital and dilute to reach escape velocity, and a shape no investor would back is a structural FAIL rather than a virtue). **The leap is full-time, not side-built** (the question is whether you can actually go full-time and survive on a founder salary, not whether you can ship in the evenings). **The target is a fund-returning exit, not a steady income** (the company has to be plausibly worth venture scale, and your *post-dilution* slice, not gross revenue and not a salary, is what has to clear your personal threshold). And **the decision hinges on a partnership** (the co-founder carries half the surface area, so these constraints score only your half; whether the other half is credibly covered is Partnership Fit's job, not this file's).

> **Make it yours.** These seven are an editable default. Reweight them, change the anchors, or swap in your own venture-scale floors. The shipped scores assume a generic VC-backed, co-founded, full-time bet.

---

## The seven, and how they relate to the solo six

The venture seven are an inversion of the solo six, not a rename of them. The mapping below is for orientation only; score the seven on their own terms.

| # | Venture constraint | Relationship to the solo set |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sustained Zone-of-Genius | Reframe of the solo Energy constraint. Scores your fit to **your half only** under the role split, not the whole job. The draining surface (the co-founder's half) is evaluated in Partnership Fit. |
| 2 | Model Curve | Kept. Same test (does every frontier release help or hurt), re-anchored to venture moats: proprietary data, a regulated or certified position, deep integration, durable customer trust. Near-gate. |
| 3 | Venture-Scale Outcome | A win-and-exit check with an explicit venture-scale path test. Two sub-checks: a fund-returnable company **and** your post-dilution slice clears your number. Near-gate. |
| 4 | Fundability & Capital Strategy | Inverts the solo Bootstrap constraint. The question is no longer "can this reach a small MRR target on my own money" but "would any investor back this shape at all, holding the team constant". |
| 5 | Motivation & Conviction Fuel | Hard gate. A fuel-durability test: is the founder here for the mission and a positive-sum value thesis, and does that fuel survive year 3. |
| 6 | Conviction & Opportunity Cost | Does conviction in *this* idea beat giving up the current path, and is the conviction the founder's own rather than borrowed from a magnetic co-founder. |
| 7 | Full-Time Commitment & Personal Runway | Hard gate. Can the founder actually go full-time: founder-salary survivability, personal runway, dependants, over the years this needs. |

Two design notes carry over. The solo Revenue-Decoupled-from-Time constraint has no standalone venture equivalent: under the venture condition value accrues to the equity stake at exit, not to passive revenue, so sub-check (b) of constraint 3 carries the personal-economics judgment. And there is deliberately **no "Founder Right-to-Win" constraint** here. Right-to-win is a single scored dimension that lives only in Partnership Fit > Combined Founder-Market Fit. Do not re-score it in this file.

## 1. Sustained Zone-of-Genius

**Question it answers:** Given the agreed role split (for example: one founder on commercial / CEO, the other on domain / product), is your average year-2 working week spent in your genius zone?

**No-double-count contract:** This constraint scores **only your half** of the company. It asks whether the slice of work that lands on you (building product, the domain, the technical core, earning user trust) is energising on an average (not best) day two years in. It does **not** ask whether the co-founder credibly covers the other half. That is Partnership Fit's question. If the role split is undefined or contested, that is a Partnership signal, not a Zone-of-Genius FAIL. Score your *intended* half here and flag the ambiguity to Partnership.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| PASS | Your half is building product, translating the domain into systems, hands-on technical work, and earning user trust. An average year-2 working week is shipping and talking to customers. The role split puts the draining surface (the raise, board management, org-building) on the co-founder. |
| Mixed | Your half is mostly genius-zone but carries a recurring drain you cannot delegate. For example, you own a slow certification process, or a meaningful slice of enterprise sales lands on you and cannot move off you to the co-founder. |
| FAIL | The role split, as actually required by the business, puts you on the draining surface most of the time (running the sales gauntlet, managing the org, owning the raise), regardless of the title on the deck. The work the company needs from you is not the work that gives you energy. |

## 2. Model Curve (near-gate)

**Question it answers:** Does every frontier model release make this venture *more* valuable, not less?

The venture must sit in a layer that improving models strengthen. The model-proof layer is proprietary data, a regulated or certified position, deep workflow integration, hardware integration, and customer trust earned over time. A FAIL here is **near-fatal**: a thin wrapper on a frontier model has no defensible venture-scale outcome once the model self-configures. If the wedge is "we connect AI tools" or "AI-powered X where X is a feature a platform will absorb", score accordingly.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| PASS | Value compounds with model capability and sits in a model-proof layer: proprietary data, a regulated or certified position, deep integration, durable customer trust. Each frontier release is a tailwind. Agents handle more edge cases for free, and the moat (data, position, trust) is untouched. |
| Mixed | Part of the wedge is model-proof but a meaningful part is a wrapper that thins with each release, or a fast-follower platform (a hyperscaler, an incumbent) could absorb the AI layer within 12-24 months and compete on distribution. |
| FAIL | Core value is the wrapper: wiring frontier models together, an interface to model capability, or "AI-powered X" where X is a commoditising feature. Every model release thins the offer. Near-fatal. |

## 3. Venture-Scale Outcome (near-gate)

**Question it answers:** Can this both return a fund **and** leave you personally above your number?

Two sub-checks, both required.

**(a) Fund-returnable.** The company can plausibly be shaped into a venture-scale outcome (roughly $300M-$1B+), not a lifestyle or services business that tops out in the low tens of millions. A great consulting practice fails this sub-check even if it is profitable.

**(b) Your post-dilution slice clears your number, even in the median-good case.** A fund-returning company can still leave a co-founder with a junior stake. Illustrative dilution maths: founder equity typically lands in a **20-40%** band at incorporation depending on the split and on a co-founder's premium as a known-fundable CEO; after two or three priced rounds, expect roughly **50-60% cumulative dilution**, leaving roughly **8-20%** at exit. On a **$300M** exit that is roughly **$24-60M**; on a **$1B** exit, roughly **$80-200M**. The risk is the bottom of the band: a junior 8% of a $300M company is roughly $24M gross, still life-changing, but if the split is unfavourable and the exit is median rather than top-end, you could end a multi-year full-time leap with a stake that is *good* but not *decisively your number*. Sub-check (b) is what feeds the master verdict's **equity-protection modulator**: a great company on (a) with a soft (b) does not automatically PURSUE.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| PASS | (a) Plausible path to a venture-scale outcome and (b) your modelled post-dilution slice in the median-good case comfortably clears your number (for example, a healthy stake at incorporation, defensible against heavy dilution, or a clear ratchet/top-up mechanism). |
| Mixed | One sub-check is soft. Either (a) the venture-scale path exists but is narrow or contested, or (b) the company can win but your split leaves you with a junior stake that only clears your number at the top end of the exit range. The equity-protection modulator should pull the verdict down. |
| FAIL | (a) Structurally a lifestyle or services shape that cannot reach venture scale, **or** (b) the split / dilution leaves you below your number even in a good exit. Either failure is near-fatal. |

## 4. Fundability & Capital Strategy

**Question it answers:** Holding the team constant, is this venture-fundable *at all, in principle*?

This is the in-principle judgment, not the operational plan. A services / consulting / agency shape that no investor would back, even with a strong team, is a structural FAIL, because the whole venture condition (dilution, full-time leap, fund-returning exit) presumes a fundable equity story. This constraint inverts the solo Bootstrap constraint: the absence of a bootstrap path is no longer a penalty, but the absence of a *fundable* path is fatal. The concrete operational capital plan (how much, from whom, the non-dilutive ladder, the cash-flow shape) lives in **Venture Feasibility > Capital Path**, not here.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| PASS | A clear venture-fundable shape: an equity story with a credible path to a large outcome, a market investors actively back, and a wedge that justifies institutional capital. A known-fundable co-founder reinforces this but is not the basis of the score. |
| Mixed | Fundable but awkward: a hardware-heavy or long-sales-cycle shape that needs a non-dilutive (grant or accelerator) ladder before it is conventionally backable, or a market only a narrow band of specialist funds will touch. Raisable, but the capital strategy is non-obvious and constrains the cap table. |
| FAIL | Structurally not venture-fundable holding the team constant: a services / consulting / body-shop shape, a market with no venture exits, or unit economics that can never justify dilution. No investor would back this shape at any team quality. |

## 5. Motivation & Conviction Fuel (hard gate)

**Question it answers:** Why are *you* here? Is it mission and positive-sum value creation, and will the fuel survive year 3?

This is the **"why you are here"** test, and it is a hard gate: a FAIL caps the idea before the 2x2 regardless of every other score. The durable fuel is a mission you actually believe in and a positive-sum value thesis. Joining to *be* a founder for the seat, the status, or proximity to a star CEO is a status game that burns out by year 3 when the work is hard and the cap table is set.

**Distinct from the Ethics gate.** This constraint is about *why you are here*. It is **not** the question of *whether the product crosses an ethical line*, which is the binary Ethics / Legal gate in [`venture-feasibility.md`](venture-feasibility.md). A product can pass Ethics (it is lawful and within the founders' line) yet fail Motivation (you are in it for the status seat), and the reverse. Score them separately.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| PASS | Your stated and revealed motivation is mission and positive-sum value: a problem you genuinely believe in or have lived. The fuel is idiosyncratic to you and plausibly survives the year-3 grind once the novelty is gone. |
| Mixed | Genuine mission pull, but mixed with status or identity motives (being a founder, proximity to a star co-founder) strong enough that year-3 durability is uncertain. Needs an explicit gut-check before committing. |
| FAIL (gate) | The primary draw is the status seat, the title, or the co-founder's halo rather than the mission. Predictably burns out by year 3. **Caps the idea before the 2x2.** |

## 6. Conviction & Opportunity Cost

**Question it answers:** Does conviction in *this specific idea* exceed the cost of giving up your current path, and is the conviction your own?

Two things have to be true. First, the conviction has to clear a high bar, because the opportunity cost is real and large: a strong current path (job, equity, career capital) is compounding, and walking away from it for a venture demands more than a good idea. It demands one you would regret *not* building. Second, the conviction must be **your own**, not borrowed. A magnetic co-founder whom multiple investors are ready to fund is easy to mistake conviction-in-the-co-founder for conviction-in-the-idea. The test isolates the idea: would you still want to build *this* if a different (still excellent) co-founder proposed it?

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| PASS | Conviction in this specific idea clearly exceeds the opportunity cost of your current path, and it is demonstrably your own. You can articulate the thesis independently and would regret not building it even with a different co-founder. |
| Mixed | Conviction is real but does not decisively beat the opportunity cost, **or** it is partly borrowed from the co-founder's reputation (the idea is attractive largely because *they* are behind it). Needs to be tested by separating the idea from the person. |
| FAIL | Conviction does not exceed the cost of leaving, or it is borrowed wholesale from the co-founder (you are excited about the partnership, not the problem). Leaving a compounding path on borrowed conviction is the classic regret case. |

## 7. Full-Time Commitment & Personal Runway (hard gate)

**Question it answers:** Can you *actually* go full-time and stay there for the years this venture needs?

A hard gate on the practical reality of the leap. Unlike the solo track, there is no moonlighting path. A venture needs full-time founders, and the leap means trading a senior salary for a founder salary for years. This constraint is about survivability, not desire: founder-salary livability against fixed costs, personal runway (a savings buffer through the dip before founder comp normalises and through the gap between raises), dependants, and household commitments over a multi-year horizon. A FAIL here caps the idea before the 2x2 no matter how good it is. Running out of personal runway mid-build is fatal regardless of the venture's quality.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| PASS | You can credibly go full-time now: founder salary plus runway covers fixed costs through the early dip and inter-round gaps, dependants and household are provided for across the multi-year horizon, and no personal-finance cliff forces an early exit. |
| Mixed | Full-time is possible but tight: it requires a specific trigger first (a closed pre-seed or seed, a runway buffer not yet in place, a household adjustment), or the personal-finance margin is thin enough that a delayed raise creates real pressure. Buildable once the trigger is met. |
| FAIL (gate) | You cannot sustainably go full-time for the duration this needs: founder salary will not cover fixed costs, runway is insufficient for the dip and inter-round gaps, or dependant / household commitments make the multi-year leap untenable now. **Caps the idea before the 2x2.** |

## Scoring Model

Each constraint scores **PASS / Mixed / FAIL**. They are not all equal.

- **Hard gates: 5 Motivation & Conviction Fuel, 7 Full-Time Commitment & Personal Runway.** A FAIL on either caps the idea **before** the 2x2 quadrant is computed. No market opportunity rescues a status-driven founder or one who runs out of personal runway. (These are the founder-side equivalent of the binary Ethics / Legal veto in Venture Feasibility.)
- **Near-gates: 2 Model Curve, 3 Venture-Scale Outcome.** A FAIL is near-fatal but not an automatic cap. A thin-wrapper FAIL or a no-venture-scale FAIL almost always sinks the idea, but the cascade may surface a reshape (a different wedge, a different cap-table structure) before a final PASS.
- **Tolerance constraints: 1 Zone-of-Genius, 4 Fundability, 6 Conviction & Opportunity Cost.** Scored into an **X/7 PASS** tally that sets the **founder-fit axis** (High vs Low) for the venture 2x2, the same way the solo six set founder fit on the solo side.

**How it feeds the Master Verdict.** Founder fit (this file) is one axis in the master-verdict cascade. The full cascade (Venture Constraints for founder fit, Market Opportunity for the band, Partnership Fit for the gate, and Venture Feasibility for CLEARED / CONDITIONAL / BLOCKED including the Ethics veto, resolving to PURSUE / SHAPE / WATCH / PASS / NO-GO) is defined in [`venture-feasibility.md`](venture-feasibility.md). One modulator rides on top: sub-check (b) of constraint 3 feeds the **equity-protection modulator** (a great company that leaves you with a junior stake pulls the verdict down toward SHAPE rather than PURSUE), and a hard-gate FAIL here forces **PASS or NO-GO** irrespective of market or partnership scores.

## Worked Example (illustrative)

*Illustrative scoring of a hypothetical horizontal-infrastructure venture under the venture condition. The numbers are illustrative only.*

| # | Constraint | Score | Justification |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sustained Zone-of-Genius | PASS | Your half is product, the technical core, and earning customer trust. The raise and org-building land on the co-founder. An average year-2 week is shipping and talking to design partners. |
| 2 | Model Curve (near-gate) | PASS | The moat is proprietary data and deep integration: model-proof. Better models make the core catch more edge cases for free, and the position is untouched by any release. |
| 3 | Venture-Scale Outcome (near-gate) | Mixed | (a) Plausibly venture-scale as the category grows. (b) Soft: if the co-founder's known-fundable-CEO premium pushes your slice down at incorporation, a median exit leaves a junior stake. The equity-protection modulator applies. |
| 4 | Fundability & Capital Strategy | PASS | A clean horizontal-infra equity story that investors actively back. A non-dilutive ladder is available; the detailed plan is deferred to Venture Feasibility > Capital Path. |
| 5 | Motivation & Conviction Fuel (gate) | PASS | A direct line to a mission you genuinely believe in. The fuel is idiosyncratic and survives year 3. Distinct from Ethics, which is scored in Venture Feasibility. |
| 6 | Conviction & Opportunity Cost | PASS | Conviction is your own: the thesis predates the partnership and you would build it with a different co-founder. It clears the cost of leaving the current path. |
| 7 | Full-Time Commitment & Personal Runway (gate) | Mixed | Full-time is viable once a pre-seed closes; until then personal runway through the founder-salary dip is tight. Buildable on the trigger of a closed round. |

**Tally:** 5/7 PASS, 2 Mixed (constraints 3 and 7), 0 FAIL. Both hard gates (5, 7) clear (7 is Mixed and trigger-gated, not FAIL, so no cap). Both near-gates resolve survivably (Model Curve PASS, Venture-Scale Mixed on sub-check (b)). **Founder fit: High**, with the equity-protection modulator flagged on constraint 3(b) to pull the master verdict toward SHAPE pending a cap table that protects your slice. The idea proceeds into the 2x2 and the full cascade in [`venture-feasibility.md`](venture-feasibility.md).
