# Partnership Fit

**Purpose.** The premise of the venture track is co-founding a VC-backed, full-time company with one specific person. When the whole bet hinges on a partnership, the partnership cannot be a paragraph of narrative buried in a README. It is scored, like any other axis, so that a weak fit shows up as a number and forces a deliberate decision rather than a hopeful one.

This is the new first-class axis that distinguishes the venture track from the solo track. It evaluates the three-way fit between an idea, you, and **this** co-founder, not co-founding in the abstract. The dimensions and weights below are reusable for any co-founder decision.

> **Make it yours.** The six dimensions and their weights are an editable default. Reweight them to match what you care about most in a partnership, but keep the weighted-composite structure and the gate bands; they are what turn a gut feeling into a decision.

---

## The Load-Bearing Asymmetry Principle

**A weak partnership DEMOTES an otherwise strong idea. It never KILLS it.**

The market opportunity belongs to the idea, not to the deal terms. If the idea is strong and the partnership is weak, the correct response is to fix the partnership (renegotiate equity, redraw roles, validate the claims, or re-team with someone else), not to walk away from the opportunity. A low Partnership Fit score is an instruction to renegotiate the deal, not to discard the venture.

The asymmetry has a hard floor and a soft floor.

- **Fragile** means "the terms are wrong, fix them first." It caps the master verdict at SHAPE. The opportunity stays live; the deal does not yet.
- **Misaligned** is the only band that forces a re-team. It says the pairing itself is broken on this idea (wrong roles, no combined right-to-win, or values that do not reconcile) and no amount of term-sheet redrafting fixes it. Even then, the *idea* may survive with a different partner. What dies is this specific partnership applied to this specific idea.

This principle is stated again at the close of the document because it is the single rule most easily forgotten under the gravity of a great-sounding co-founder.

## The Six Dimensions

Score each 1-5. No half points. Justify in one line. The composite is a **weighted** average.

| # | Dimension | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Roles & Complementarity | 20% |
| 2 | Combined Founder-Market Fit | 20% |
| 3 | Equity & Commitment Alignment | 20% |
| 4 | Investor Access as an Asset | 15% |
| 5 | Trust & Working History | 15% |
| 6 | Break Conditions / Founder Pre-nup | 10% |

---

### 1. Roles & Complementarity (20%)

Is there a clean split (one side commercial / CEO, the other domain / product) where the two skill sets cover the founding surface with no critical gap and minimal overlap?

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 5 | A clean, explicit split both agree on. The skills cover the whole surface (commercial, product, domain, fundraising). No critical gap, almost no overlap. |
| 4 | A clear split with one negotiated overlap or one minor gap covered by an early hire. |
| 3 | The split is workable but undefined in places, or both gravitate to the same surface (for example, both want product). |
| 2 | A material gap on a founding-critical surface, or an unresolved contest over who owns it. |
| 1 | No split. Two people for one job and a hole where a co-founder should be. |

**Ask:** Could each of you describe the other's lane in one sentence, and would those two sentences not collide?

---

### 2. Combined Founder-Market Fit (20%)

Does **this pair** uniquely win **this idea**, more than either could alone and more than a generic strong team?

**This is the sole home of "right-to-win" in the entire venture system.** Right-to-win is scored here and nowhere else. Each founder brings a stack (a mix of domain credibility, technical depth, commercial leadership, fundraising reach, prior 0->1 experience). The question is whether the *product* of those two stacks is a defensible edge on *this specific idea*, not whether either stack is impressive on its own. Venture Feasibility deliberately does not re-score advantage; advantage is scored here.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 5 | This pair is close to the best-possible founding team for this idea. Both stacks are load-bearing and compounding. |
| 4 | A strong combined fit; both founders' edges are directly used by the idea, with one edge clearly dominant. |
| 3 | A good team, but the right-to-win comes mostly from one founder; the other is competent-generic for this idea. |
| 2 | Neither stack is a true edge on this idea; the pairing wins on hustle, not on unfair advantage. |
| 1 | The idea actively wastes both stacks. The edges each founder brings are irrelevant to it. |

**Ask:** Why this pair, on this idea, rather than any other well-funded two-person team?

---

### 3. Equity & Commitment Alignment (20%)

Are the equity split, vesting, control, and full-time commitment acceptable to you, not merely tolerable but a deal you would happily take again?

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 5 | The split, vesting, and control all sit where you would happily sign. Both are full-time, all-in, on the same time horizon. |
| 4 | Terms are fair with one open point you are confident will close in your favour. |
| 3 | Terms are live and negotiable; the likely landing zone is acceptable but not yet agreed. |
| 2 | A real misalignment exists (a lopsided split, founder-unfriendly control, asymmetric commitment) that must be fixed before any yes. |
| 1 | Terms you would not accept, or a commitment asymmetry (one full-time, one hedging) that poisons the partnership. |

This dimension feeds the equity-protection modulator in the master verdict: a fund-returning exit on a thin slice can be a worse personal outcome than a smaller exit on a fair one.

**Ask:** Would you sign these terms knowing the company succeeds, and knowing it fails?

---

### 4. Investor Access as an Asset (15%)

Do the co-founder's specific investor relationships materially de-risk **this** raise, not raises in general?

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 5 | Named, warm, stage-and-thesis-appropriate investors, independently validated, who have signalled readiness to back the co-founder. |
| 4 | A strong validated network with clear paths to the right funds for this idea's stage and domain. |
| 3 | A credible network, but its relevance to *this* idea's stage or thesis is unconfirmed, or the relationships are warm-but-untested. |
| 2 | A network exists but is mismatched to the raise (wrong stage, wrong thesis, wrong geography). |
| 1 | The claimed access does not survive contact with diligence. |

**Validate before you score.** Score this dimension on validated relationships. Where the co-founder's claimed investor interest and reputation are self-reported and not yet independently confirmed, mark the score provisional and do not let an unverified network inflate the composite. The discipline is the same as anywhere else in the system: an unconfirmed load-bearing claim is provisional until a primary source confirms it.

**Ask:** Which named funds, at which stage, have signalled they would back this, and who confirmed it other than the co-founder?

---

### 5. Trust & Working History (15%)

Is there a real prior working relationship, a clean reputation, and evidence of how this pair handles disagreement under pressure?

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 5 | A deep prior working history, including hard moments handled well. The reputation is independently verified. You would put your own capital behind their character. |
| 4 | A solid working history and good reputation; disagreements have been handled, if not under founding-level stress. |
| 3 | Known and liked, but never tested together under real pressure or shared accountability. |
| 2 | Mostly social or second-hand knowledge; the reputation is strong but the working history is thin. |
| 1 | An untested relationship, or a reputation or behaviour flag that diligence has surfaced. |

**Ask:** When this pair last disagreed with real stakes, what happened?

---

### 6. Break Conditions / Founder Pre-nup (10%)

Are the mechanics for things going wrong defined in advance: vesting and cliff, IP assignment, departure process, the pivot tie-break, key-person dependency, and any decay clock on the opportunity itself?

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 5 | All pre-nup mechanics agreed in writing before signing: vesting and cliff, IP assignment, departure terms, pivot tie-break, key-person mitigation, and a known decision deadline. |
| 4 | Mechanics agreed in principle; paperwork pending but no open disputes. |
| 3 | Mechanics discussed but several left to "we'll sort it later." |
| 2 | Key mechanics unaddressed, or an unmitigated key-person dependency on the co-founder. |
| 1 | No pre-nup, an unbounded key-person risk, or a decay clock already forcing a decision before diligence can finish. |

Two failure modes this dimension exists to catch.

- **Key-person dependency.** If one founder is a single point of failure for fundraising and the CEO seat and they walk, can the company still raise and lead? An unmitigated yes-it-dies is a score of 2 or below.
- **Partnership-opportunity decay.** The option to co-found with this person is not free to hold. Is there a date by which they need a decision (another co-founder in the wings, a fund timeline, a competing offer)? A hard, near deadline that pre-empts proper diligence is itself a low score, not a reason to rush.

**Ask:** If they disappear in month nine, what survives, and what is the last date they can be told no?

## Composite and Gate Bands

A weighted average of the six dimensions, mapped to one gate band. The band modulates the master verdict per the asymmetry principle; it never overrides the idea's own opportunity.

| Composite | Gate Band | What it does to the verdict |
|---|---|---|
| >= 4.0 | **Strong** | No partnership drag. The verdict is set by the idea and the feasibility. Proceed on merits. |
| 3.0 - 3.9 | **Workable** | The partnership holds with named fixes. The verdict proceeds; carry the open items as conditions. |
| 2.0 - 2.9 | **Fragile** | Fix the terms first. **Caps the master verdict at SHAPE.** The opportunity stays live, the deal does not, until the weak dimensions are renegotiated. |
| < 2.0 | **Misaligned** | Forces a re-team. The pairing is broken for this idea; the idea may survive with a different partner, this partnership does not. |

The weighted composite is `0.20·D1 + 0.20·D2 + 0.20·D3 + 0.15·D4 + 0.15·D5 + 0.10·D6`.

## Validate Before You Score

Before any Partnership Fit score is recorded, independently validate the co-founder's load-bearing claims, especially investor readiness (Dimension 4) and reputation (Dimension 5). Until that validation is done, those scores are **provisional** and the composite must be flagged as such in the idea README. The whole track leans on the co-founder's contribution, and an unverified claim there silently inflates the most important axis in the system.

Validation means primary sources: talk to one or two of the named funds or people who have worked with the co-founder, not just the co-founder's account of their own standing. This is ordinary scoring discipline, not a special-case rule, and it applies until a specific claim is confirmed.

## Founder Pre-nup: Diligence Questions to Answer Before Signing

Answer all of these, in writing, before equity is allocated.

- **Control:** Who is CEO? Who breaks a tie on a pivot when the two founders disagree on direction?
- **Equity:** What is the split? What vesting schedule and cliff? What happens to unvested and vested equity if one founder leaves, voluntarily or fired?
- **IP:** Is all founder IP (including pre-existing work and hard-won know-how) assigned to the company cleanly?
- **Hard-nos:** What is each person's non-negotiable line: the ethics or legal boundary, off-limits domains, fundraising terms each will not accept? Do these reconcile?
- **Key-person mitigation:** If a founder who is a single point of failure for fundraising and the CEO seat exits, what is the plan that keeps the company fundable and led?
- **Decay clock:** By what date does the co-founder need a decision, and does that date leave room for honest diligence, or is it forcing a yes?

---

## In Closing: The Asymmetry, Restated

A weak Partnership Fit score is never a kill signal for the idea. **Fragile means fix the terms first** and caps the verdict at SHAPE; **only Misaligned forces a re-team.** A low score is an instruction to renegotiate the deal, not to discard the opportunity.

Partnership Fit feeds the partnership-modulation step and the equity-protection modulator of the master-verdict cascade in `venture-feasibility.md`.
